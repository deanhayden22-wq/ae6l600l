4. WHAT IT IS *NOT* -- the transient hypothesis fails its own test
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The obvious read of section 3 is tip-in / accel enrichment ([0xFFFF7B8C], which
ffb_command_chain_trace.txt showed is [7B90] + [7B94]). Tested and it does not hold
up on the instantaneous-rate form:

    residual vs dMAF/dt, flat region:
        -100..-50   n= 5  med +0.0708
         -50..  +0  n=18  med +0.0625
          +0.. +50  n=49  med +0.0347
         +50..+100  n=19  med +0.0747
        +100..+150  n=12  med +0.0688
        +150..+200  n=23  med +0.0708
        +200..+250  n= 8  med +0.0823
    -> no monotone relationship.

Full Pearson scan over every logged channel (OL, load >= 1.0, ECT > 170 F,
n = 1919). NOTHING dominates:
    Timing  -0.267 | dRPM +0.232 | dMAF +0.202 | Throttle +0.191 | ECT -0.161
    avcs    -0.148 | RPM   -0.135| wgdc +0.121 | load +0.112 | MPH +0.092
    ol_s    +0.091 | IAT   -0.086| MAF +0.026 | IDC -0.026 | IPW -0.014 | MAP -0.003

INFERRED, weakest link named: `load` is a MAF-derived instantaneous proxy while the
ECU's own load is filtered, so a genuine ramp-rate dependence would be partly
smeared by the proxy. The scan rules out a strong single-channel driver; it does
not rule out the transient family outright.

Worth carrying: descriptor 0xAD208 (20-byte 1-D form) decodes to a FLAT 0.100:
    0AD208: 00 07 08 00 00 0C E8 98 00 0C E8 B4 39 00 00 00 C0 80 00 00
    n=7, uint16, axis 0xCE898 = 800..3200 RPM, data 0xCE8B4, scale 1/8192, off -4.0
    0CE8B4: 83 33 x7 -> 33587/8192 - 4 = 0.09998 at every RPM point.
    Sibling 0xAD21C -> 0xCE8E0 = 20 00 x7 -> a flat 1.0000.
HUNCH (55%): a term capped near 0.100 is doing most of the work, and 0xCE8B4 is it.
What triggers it: 0.100 sits right inside the observed 0..0.118 band and the table
is flat, which matches a gap that appears at full amplitude rather than scaling.
Cheapest test: section 6.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. THE IPW CHAIN RUN BACKWARDS -- it closes to +/-1%, so FFB IS the real command
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This was the "or maybe lets see how the ipw is determined and run it backwards"
half of the question. Result: the loop closes, which makes the section-3 gap real
fuel rather than a display artifact.

Model:  air per cylinder per cycle = load/2 grams
        fuel per cylinder          = K * (IPW_ms - deadtime) * percyl_multiplier
        AFR_ipw                    = air / fuel

VERIFIED constant: Injector Flow Scaling @0xCBE0C = 45 99 A0 00 = float 4916.0
    ECUFlash display = 2707090 / 4916 = 550.669 cc/min  (stock, unchanged in 19c)
    -> K = 550.669 * 0.745 / 60000 = 0.006837 g/ms

Independent check on the whole model: fitting K and deadtime by OLS from 15,998
steady closed-loop rows (CL/OL==8, ECT>170 F, load/RPM/IPW flat over +/-3 samples)
with no ROM input at all gives
    fuel_per_cyl_g = 0.006661 * (IPW_ms - 0.7359)   ->  536.5 cc/min
** 536.5 vs the ROM's own 550.669 is 2.6%. The logged IPW channel, the derived
   load channel and the ROM injector constant are mutually consistent. **

Fitting deadtime alone against the wideband gives 0.720 ms, and AFR_ipw vs wbo2 on
those steady CL rows is mean +0.36% (sd 7.07%, n=7958).

Now OL. With NO per-cylinder term the reconstruction drifts LEAN with load:
    IDC  0-20  n=870  AFR_ipw vs FFB  median -2.17%
    IDC 20-40  n=766                  median +0.51%
    IDC 40-60  n=186                  median +5.55%
    IDC 60-80  n= 65                  median +5.36%
    IDC 80-100 n= 32                  median +4.27%

The break sits at IDC ~30, i.e. IPW ~7-8 ms at those RPM. That is exactly where the
Per Injector Pulse Width Compensation tables step. VERIFIED, decoded fresh:
    A 0xD07E8 / B 0xD0994 / C 0xD0B40 / D 0xD0CEC, each 17 x 16 uint8,
    scaling (x*0.78125)-100, X = Last Calculated Base PW 1000..16000 (us),
    Y = Engine Speed 700..5600.
    Row 12 (4400 rpm), top PW column:
        A 0xD07E8+204 -> 0x7A = 122 -> -4.6875 %
        B 0xD0994+204 -> 0x78 = 120 -> -6.2500 %
        C 0xD0B40+204 -> 0x7A = 122 -> -4.6875 %
        D 0xD0CEC+204 -> 0x7B = 123 -> -3.9062 %
        mean = -4.8828 %
    At 4400-5600 rpm the mean of A-D is 0.00% for PW columns 1000-6000 us and
    -4.88% for columns 8000 us and up. Our WOT IPW is 20-23 ms, i.e. past the top
    of the axis, so it clamps to the -4.88% column.

Testing the two possible sign conventions -- refit deadtime under each, then look
at the OL reconstruction error:

    convention                             deadtime   IDC 20-40  40-60  60-80  80-100
    no comp at all                          0.720 ms   +0.51%   +5.55% +5.36%  +4.27%
    PW * (1 + c/100)   (c=-4.88 -> less)    0.749 ms   +1.47%  +11.15% +10.97% +9.28%
    PW * (1 - c/100)   (c=-4.88 -> MORE)    0.685 ms   -0.69%   +0.50%  +0.25%  +0.94%

** VERIFIED by fit: applied_PW = base_PW * (1 - comp/100). A negative table entry
   LENGTHENS the pulse. Under that convention the IPW chain reconstructs FFB to
   within +/-1% at every duty band from 20% to 100%. **

Consequences:
  * FFB is not a display quantity. The low-10s command is really being injected,
    and the logged IPW channel is the COMMON value -- the per-injector tables add
    ~4.9% more fuel at WOT that never appears in the IPW column.
  * The wideband agrees: wbo2 tracks FFB within +/-0.5 AFR across every IDC band,
    while un-compensated AFR_ipw is 0.5-0.6 AFR lean of both.
  * This directly touches the injector-swap worksheet. docs/injector-rescale.md
    calls the Per Injector PW Comp axis question "-3% to +6% of load-dependent,
    per-channel error". Measured here it is a clean 0 -> -4.88% step, i.e. +4.9%
    fuel, and today the WOT operating point is CLAMPED off the top of the axis.
    Rescaling that axis by k=0.55 pulls a 21 ms pulse to ~11.5 ms equivalent --
    still off the top of a stock axis, so the clamp survives the swap. Confirm
    before flashing new injectors.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. WHAT IS STILL OPEN, AND THE ONE MOVE THAT SETTLES IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Eliminated this session: the OL map term (sec 1), Timing Comp (sec 1c), the whole
minimum-enrichment floor path (sec 2), and any IPW/injector-model artifact (sec 5).

Still standing, from ffb_command_chain_trace.txt Part 3:
    base extras : [73B4] [7A08] [7A14] [7AF4] [7B8C]
    tail        : - [7A44] + [7BCC] + [7BA8]
[7A08] is ECT-seeded and decays to zero, so it is out at 167-178 F. That leaves six
addends and no way to separate them from the logged channels -- which is exactly
why section 4's correlation scan came back flat.

THE MOVE: log the addends directly. No ROM change is needed. The SSM read-addresses
patch at 0x09A3C2-0x09A473 (docs/ssm-read-patch.md) already supports 4-byte tagged
RAM reads -- tag 0xF4, precedent `0xF465FC` -> 4 bytes at 0xFFFF65FC. So add to
logs/logcfg.txt:

    0xF4730C   the FFB source itself (phi, float) -- validates the whole decode
    0xF4798C   the OL map result -- confirms sec 1 on the car
    0xF47B8C   transient / tip-in sum
    0xF47BCC   gated enrichment
    0xF47A14   unnamed base addend
    0xF47AF4   unnamed base addend (no live writer found)
    0xF473B4   unnamed base addend (no writer found; also SSM-packed at 0xFFFF6854)

One WOT pull with those seven channels decomposes the sum arithmetically and the
question is finished. Caveat before wiring it: every extended channel currently in
logcfg.txt uses the 0xFF tag, none uses 0xF4 or 0xF2, so the 4-byte path is
UNEXERCISED on this setup. Prove it on 0xF4730C first -- its value must equal
14.7/FFB row for row. If that matches, the other six are trustworthy.

Also still open and worth a line: `load` is derived (MAF*60/RPM), not the ECU's own
figure. docs/ssm-read-patch.md names 0xFFFF65FC as engine load. Logging 0xF465FC
alongside would remove the proxy from every OL analysis in this project, not just
this one.
