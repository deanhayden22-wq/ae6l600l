# 20.10 → 20.12 outside-the-box data exploration

**Scope:** the 14 logs mapped to revs 20.10/20.11/20.12 in `logs/rom_rev_map.csv`. 754,534 rows. 503 minutes of drive time.
- 20.10: 2 logs (4-27, 5-2) — 82 min
- 20.11: 6 logs (5-8 → 5-12) — 77 min
- 20.12: 6 logs (5-17/log0002–log0007) — 344 min, highway-skewed (median MPH 73 vs 39–41 on prior revs)

**Method:** load all 14 logs into a single parquet, run 11 angle-scans, rank by signal strength, drill the standout, defend both top findings against alternative explanations.

## Top findings

### 1. Stutter rate fingerprint (drill target)

Definition of a "stutter": throttle plate drops ≥3% in one sample while the pedal is held (Accelerator>5%, |Δapp|<1.5%, prior sample also pedal-applied, RPM>1500). This is ECU-commanded throttle drop, not driver pedal release.

After normalizing to a common operating envelope (RPM 1500–4500, load 0.3–1.5, MPH 15–65) and restricting to pedal-modulating segments (3-second APP std > 5%) to avoid the "cruise has no pedal motion" tautology:

| Rev   | Pedal-mod min | Stutters | Rate /min | 95% CI (bootstrap)   |
|-------|---------------|----------|-----------|----------------------|
| 20.10 | 4.8           | 1        | 0.21      | 0.00 – 0.68          |
| 20.11 | 6.4           | 10       | **1.57**  | 0.69 – 2.58          |
| 20.12 | 14.5          | 4        | 0.28      | 0.07 – 0.57          |

**The 20.11 stutter rate is ~6× higher than 20.10 and 20.12, with non-overlapping 95% CIs.** Whatever changed between 20.10 → 20.11 introduced a driveability regression that was then resolved on 20.12. Stutter severity (depth of throttle drop when it happens) is similar across revs (p50 -3 to -4%); the rev-to-rev difference is in *frequency*, not magnitude.

20.12 stutter mechanism breakdown (n=19, broader filter):
- 63% co-incident with `Trgt_Boost` step-down within the same window
- 47% with `mrp > Trgt_Boost + 0.5 psi` (boost overshoot)
- ~21% in boost (mrp > 2 psi)

The deepest 20.12 events (e.g. 5-17_log0007 sample 5799, APP 66%, RPM 4636, mrp 7.4 climbing to 12) show boost-management cutting throttle as mrp overshoots target — Tdp drops 10.25 → 9.64 right after the throttle pull. This is real torque-management activity under load, not an artifact.

The lower-RPM 20.12 events (1500–3000, low load) are tip-in feathering — driver oscillating between 10–30% pedal, throttle filter overshoots and trims back. Different mechanism, smaller magnitude.

### 2. AFR target moved on 20.12 but the engine didn't follow (defended)

At load band 1.0–1.3 (cruise-to-mid-load):

| Rev   | FFB target | Measured wbo2 | Residual (wbo2 − FFB) | n (CL=8) |
|-------|------------|---------------|------------------------|----------|
| 20.10 | 14.04      | ~14.08        | +0.04                  | 3,097    |
| 20.11 | 13.81      | ~14.19        | +0.38                  | 2,528    |
| 20.12 | **13.62**  | ~14.13        | **+0.51**              | **23,255**|

Critical detail uncovered during defend: **measured AFR is essentially constant across all three revs (~14.1)**. What changed is the commanded AFR — it moved 0.4 leaner-to-richer (14.04 → 13.62) between 20.10 and 20.12. The engine is not following the richer target. Possible causes (in order of likelihood):

1. MAF reading high in this load region → fuel calc thinks more air than reality
2. AFC trim saturated against the target — closed loop has nowhere to go
3. Load band straddles CL/OL boundary; OL enrichment table delivers same fueling regardless of FFB

Note: A 160ms cross-correlation peak was found between FFB and wbo2 (not 320ms as previously noted in memory). All residual numbers above use the corrected 4-sample lag.

### 3. Cruise RPM variance halved

| Rev   | Cruise RPM std |
|-------|----------------|
| 20.10 | 364            |
| 20.11 | 368            |
| 20.12 | **171**        |

53% reduction in RPM oscillation under cruise. Consistent with the cruise-tuning work memory tracks for 20.12; surfaced here as a quantified outcome.

## Data hygiene notes (apply to future runs)

- **WB cross-correlation lag is 160ms (4 samples) at 25 Hz**, not 320ms. Memory entry `reference_logger_columns.md` lists 320ms — that's stale or wrong. Verified on 77k cruise samples from 20.12.
- **20.12 corpus is dominated by two long highway drives** (log0002 = 145 min active, log0007 = 127 min active). Any per-rev comparison without controlling for drive context will be confounded.
- **Sub-1-sample log dt gaps:** 20.10 logs (4-27, 5-2) contain key-off pauses (dt std 194s / 67s). Filter out gaps > 1s before time-domain analysis.
- The `iam_drop_events` metric is unreliable for 20.12 because logs start with IAM already at 0 from a prior drive — no in-log transition is captured.

## Recommended next moves

1. **MAF / fuel residual at 1.0–1.3 load (mid-load)** — pull the AFC saturation distribution at the affected cells, check whether the residual is bounded by trim authority or by MAF curve shape. The injector-upgrade work in the pipeline (`project_injector_upgrade.md`) will require fixing this region anyway.
2. **20.11 vs 20.12 binary diff** — what specifically changed that fixed the stutter regression? Memory notes 20.12 = "1st 20.12 log" but doesn't attribute the stutter fix. Worth pinning to a specific table change for the changelog.
3. **DFCO exit AFC bump on 20.12** — small but consistent (-0.78 median). May tie to the closed-loop residual issue above (same trim deficit showing up at DFCO recovery).
